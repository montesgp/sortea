# ADR 0017: Mercado Pago account history and switching while selling

Status: accepted, 2026-10-29. Amends the 2026-10-17 amendment of ADR 0004 (Tenant secrets): switching to another merchant is no longer refused while payments are in progress.

## Context

ADR 0004's 2026-10-17 amendment kept one Mercado Pago account per organization. Orders did not record which account they were created with, and webhooks and reconciliation used the active account's credentials. So switching to another merchant was refused while any order awaited payment or any payment was being credited.

The owner needs to switch during an open campaign (odd/tasks/mercadopago-account-switch.md):

- S1: "mientras existe alguna campania abierta podemos querer cambiar la cuenta de mercado pago de destino desde el backoffice y todo deberia seguir funcionando correctamente."
- S2: "Si justo en el momento del cambio alguien compra, la compra se ejecuta contra la definida en ese momento, pero el proximo usuario ya pagaria a la cuenta que se acaba de cambiar."
- S3: "Eso solo pueden cambiarlo sysadmin por organizations."
- S5: "En mi caso voy a cambiar de [la cuenta del primer cliente] a otra cuenta en medio de la campania." Traffic is low, but correctness must not depend on timing.
- S6: "La cuenta anterior no se deberia borrar excepto por confirmacion, porque el admin podria querer volver a esa cuenta."

## Decision

- **An account is a merchant (collector id) in a mode.** Saving credentials for the same pair again is a rotation and updates that account. Any other pair creates a new account.
- **Retire, never overwrite.** Connecting another account marks the previous one retired (`retired_at`) and keeps its sealed credentials. One account per organization is active (a partial unique index). Only the active account takes new purchases.
- **Orders are pinned to their account.** Each order records the account it was created with (`orders.payment_integration_id`). Checkout takes the organization row `FOR SHARE` before reading the active account; connect and disconnect take it `FOR UPDATE`. A purchase that overlaps a switch therefore ends on the previous account, and the next one uses the new account. Migration 0016 assigns existing orders to the organization's account when it had exactly one; other legacy orders stay unassigned and are matched by the signing account's collector.
- **Going back reuses the stored credentials** (S6). "Volver a usar esta cuenta" makes a previous account active again without retyping its credentials. Mercado Pago checks them first and they must still answer for the stored merchant; a refusal (400, 401, 403) or another merchant changes nothing, and the console asks to connect the account again. Activation then holds the organization row `FOR UPDATE` like a switch, retires the current account and audits `integration.mercadopago_reactivated`.
- **The webhook account comes from the signature.** A notification is checked against every one of the organization's accounts, active first, each comparison timing-safe; when none validates it, the answer is 401. Accounts configured with the same secret all validate it, so the payment is read with each signing account in turn, the account of the payment's recorded order first, until one returns it; a provider error from one of them does not stop the search, and the answer is 503 only when none returned it and at least one failed. That account's payment must belong to an order of that account (`merchant_mismatch` otherwise). Refunds and chargebacks arrive the same way, through the account that took the payment.
- **Reconciliation runs per account.** It searches the active account and every retired account with orders created within the last 180 days (`RETIRED_ACCOUNT_HORIZON_MS`), merging results by payment id. Refunds and chargebacks reach a payment weeks or months after the purchase, and a lost webhook for one of them is found only by searching the account that took the payment; the provider export uses the same rule. A retired account that Mercado Pago refuses becomes the critical item `account_unavailable` ("Cuenta anterior sin acceso"), which clears once the account answers again.
- **Disconnecting deletes every account.** "Desvincular cuenta" deletes the active and the retired accounts with their credentials. It is refused while any of them has payments in progress: orders awaiting payment, or payments `pending` or `in_process`. Orders keep their history; their account reference becomes empty.
- **Only platform staff switch accounts** (S3). The routes require a platform admin, and the console page is limited to them; organization roles get 403.

## Consequences

- A switch takes effect immediately, with no waiting window. The platform console explains that purchases already started are charged, confirmed and reconciled with the account they were created with.
- Each account's webhook must stay configured in Mercado Pago with the organization's notification URL, the previous account's included, until its payments, refunds and chargebacks are settled. The setup guide lists the steps (docs/launch/mercadopago-setup.md, "Switching accounts during a campaign").
- Retired credentials stay sealed in the database until the organization disconnects Mercado Pago. Revoking them in Mercado Pago shows up as `account_unavailable` in reconciliation instead of failing silently.
- Each webhook runs one signature check per account, and accounts sharing a secret may cost one extra payment read. That cost is negligible at the expected number of accounts per organization.
- 180 days is the platform's horizon for late refunds and chargebacks. A retired account whose orders are all older is no longer searched; its payments are re-read only when the platform touches them or they have an open item.
- A retired account's webhook secret can be updated in place since 2026-11-10 (amendment below). Reactivating an account keeps its stored secret.
- Going back to a previous account depends on its stored credentials still working. Revoked credentials are found before anything changes, and the account then has to be connected again with new ones.

## Amendment 2026-11-10: updating an account's credentials in place

The owner, in production: "no puedo editar la integracion de mercado pago ni tampoco coincide el nombre clave de notificaciones con lo que propone mercado pago". An account had been connected with the application's Client Secret where the console asked for the webhook secret, so every notification would fail its signature and payments would wait for reconciliation.

- **Update without switching.** `PATCH /platform/organizations/:id/integrations/mercadopago/:integrationId` replaces the access token, the webhook secret or both of one account, active or retired; at least one is required. The account keeps its row, mode, active or retired state and orders; its version increases.
- **Same merchant only.** A new token is checked with Mercado Pago first and must answer for the account's collector id. Another merchant is refused with 409 `collector_mismatch`: that is a switch, made with "Cambiar de cuenta". A checked token refreshes the account's status and kind (`testUser`, `simulator`) as a verification does. A secret-only update calls no provider, since Mercado Pago cannot check a webhook secret.
- **Locks and audit.** The update holds the organization row `FOR UPDATE`, like a switch, and reads the stored credentials again under the lock before sealing the merged pair. It audits `integration.mercadopago.credentials_updated` with the fields that changed (`accessToken`, `webhookSecret`), never their values.
- **The secret is checked by the next notification.** The organization detail carries `lastWebhook`, the time, topic, outcome and signature validity of the last recorded webhook (`payment_events`), without the order id or the payload. The console shows it on the active account so staff can confirm the secret after "Simular notificación" in Mercado Pago. It is the organization's last notification: a valid signature means one of its accounts' secrets matched.
- **A token pasted as the secret is refused.** A webhook secret starting with `APP_USR-` or `TEST-` is refused with 400 `invalid_webhook_secret`, when connecting and when updating. The Client Secret has no recognizable shape and cannot be refused this way; the console names the field "Firma secreta del webhook", as Mercado Pago does, and says it is not the Client Secret.

## Amendment 2026-11-11: backup account, per-account verification and a code to switch

The owner, in production (odd/tasks/console-polish-2026-11-11.md, S4-S6): the second account "debe poder quedar configurada tal como la otra y debe quedar simple el switch account"; "antes de switchear deberiamos tener un bloqueo para confirmacion de seguridad"; "el verificar conexion debe ser independiente [...] antes de switchear quiero testear si esta ok la app y configs". Decisions L2 and L3 of that document: the second account is the "Cuenta de respaldo", and the lock is the platform admin's current authenticator code.

- **Three roles, no new columns.** The principal is the active account (`is_active`). The backup is an account that is neither active nor retired (`is_active` false, `retired_at` null); a previous account is retired (`retired_at` set). At most one backup per organization and provider: connecting another backup retires the current one, under the organization row lock like every switch. The organization detail names each account's `role` (`principal`, `backup`, `previous`).
- **Connecting a backup** (`PUT /platform/organizations/:id/integrations/mercadopago` with `role: "backup"`) validates like connecting the principal: Mercado Pago checks the token, the merchant is recorded, the secret is sealed and a token pasted as the secret is refused. It needs a principal (409 `principal_required`), refuses the principal's own merchant and mode (409 `already_principal`), and the same backup merchant and mode again rotates its credentials. Audited as `integration.mercadopago_backup_connected`, with the replaced backup when there was one. Checkout, webhooks and reconciliation are unchanged: only the principal takes new purchases, and the backup is one more account whose signature and orders are checked.
- **The principal no longer changes by connecting.** With a principal, connecting another merchant or mode as the principal is refused with 409 `switch_requires_backup`. Only the first account and a rotation of the principal itself (same merchant and mode, which never changes who gets paid) are accepted without a code. This supersedes "Any other pair creates a new account" and the "Cambiar de cuenta" form of the decision above.
- **Every change or removal of who gets paid needs the code.** `POST .../:integrationId/activate` and "Desvincular" (`DELETE /platform/organizations/:id/integrations/mercadopago`) take `{ code }`, the signed-in platform admin's current TOTP code, with the second factor's rules (docs/spec/SECURITY.md, "Segundo factor"): one step either side, each step once (the last accepted step is shared with the sign-in), and the same per-user limit of 5 wrong codes in 15 minutes, on the same counter. An admin without a confirmed authenticator app gets 409 `totp_not_configured`; a wrong or replayed code 400 `invalid_code`, a locked counter 429; none of them changes anything or reaches Mercado Pago. Then activation checks the stored credentials as before (they must answer for the stored merchant), and disconnecting checks the payments in progress as before, under the organization row lock. Disconnecting needs the code because a disconnect followed by a first connection would otherwise change who gets paid. The first connection of an organization with no account is the only code-free path that sets who gets paid, and it cannot replace a merchant because there is none; a rotation of the principal (same merchant and mode) changes credentials, never the merchant. `integration.mercadopago_disconnected` records `confirmedWith: "totp"`. This supersedes the checkbox confirmation of "Desvincular cuenta" in the decision above.
- **"Usar como principal" swaps roles.** On the backup, the backup becomes the principal and the former principal becomes the backup (not retired), so going back is the same action. Audited as `integration.mercadopago_promoted` with who switched, from which account (`previousIntegrationId`, `previousCollectorId`) to which, and `confirmedWith: "totp"`, never the code. "Volver a usar esta cuenta" on a previous account keeps its behaviour (the principal is retired, the backup stays) and needs the code too; it is still audited as `integration.mercadopago_reactivated`. Orders stay pinned as above: purchases started before the switch keep their account.
- **"Verificar conexión" per account.** `POST .../:integrationId/verify` checks the stored credentials of the principal, the backup or a previous account and records the result (`status`, `lastVerifiedAt`, `lastError`, account kind) on that account only, never changing which one charges. `POST .../verify` without an account still checks the principal. The audit `integration.mercadopago_verified` carries the account's role.
- **Which account signed each notification.** Migration 0022 adds `payment_events.integration_id`: the account whose webhook secret validated the signature (the principal first when several share it), or the account that had the payment once read; null for a rejected signature and for notifications recorded before. It has no foreign key, since `payment_events` is append-only and the id must outlive "Desvincular", like an audit target. Each account in the organization detail carries `lastWebhook`, the last notification its secret validated, so the backup's application can be checked with "Simular notificación" before it charges; the organization-wide `lastWebhook`, rejected signatures included, stays once in the shared notifications box.
- **Console.** The Mercado Pago tab uses the screen's width: principal and backup side by side on wide screens, each with its mode, status, merchant, last notification, "Verificar conexión" and "Actualizar credenciales"; the backup has "Usar como principal" in two steps (what will happen, then the code) and "Reemplazar por otra cuenta". An empty slot offers "Conectar cuenta de respaldo". Previous accounts and the notification URL stay as they were; "Desvincular cuenta" takes the same two steps as "Usar como principal" (what will happen, then the code).

Consequences: switching takes two steps (connect the backup, then use it) and an authenticator app; a platform admin without one cannot switch until they set it up in "Mi cuenta". A stolen session alone can neither redirect payments nor remove the accounts to connect another one: it would also need the current code, with at most 5 guesses per 15 minutes. Accounts that share a webhook secret attribute their notifications to the principal first.
